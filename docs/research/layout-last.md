# What was left of the editor's hand-built nodes after G-85

Researched 2026-11-14 for issue #97 (G-86), asked by bevy_dark_factory (the Cauldron), #260 (UI-99), tracking #208. After the editor adopted G-84 and G-85 its raw `Node` literals and hand patches went from 230 to 80; what was left that gooey can solve is here. Each item is a general need; the editor is the example and its name is in no API.

## Method

The rules of G-84 (`docs/research/layout-gaps.md`) stand: a layout choice is data on the node, never a literal in the host and never a number in `apply_skin`; the constructor or component puts it on the node and `apply_skin` writes the `Node`, so it follows the skin and the player's text size. G-84's review added one: **every field is an `Option`, and a field left `None` leaves the node alone** (`RowLayout`), so a component sets only what it names and never resets what a role or a host gave. Components that set a size, a space or a shape go on any node with a `Role`; where a unit is needed it is the skin's own (a step of `GooeySkin::spacing`, a multiple of the line weight `GooeySkin::border`, a step of `GooeySkin::radii`). Pointer defaults are documented on each constructor (a mark or a gap is `Pickable::IGNORE` like `screen()`).

## Items
2. **Padding and margin off the skin's scale: `Space::Px(f32)`.** `Space` was `Zero` or a `Step` of `GooeySkin::spacing`; the editor's chip (6 by 3), button, document tab (12 by 8), follow chip, folded palette header and cost readout are measures that are not on the scale. `Space::Px(f32)` is a literal logical px, written by `apply_skin` like the others and finite-checked: below zero is zero, a number that is not finite leaves its side alone (`Space::resolve`, `a_px_space_is_finite_checked_and_never_negative`). It does **not** follow the skin's scale (`a_px_space_does_not_follow_the_skin_scale`) or the text size, which is the point and the cost: prefer a `Step` where one will do. `Space` is no longer `Eq` (an `f32`).
5. **`Extent::Vh`, `Vw`, `VMin`, `VMax`.** The frame root's height is `Val::Vh(100.0)`, which `Extent` could not say. Four new variants, each a percentage of the viewport (`Vh(100.0)` is the window's whole height), written as Bevy's own `Val::Vh`/`Vw`/`VMin`/`VMax` through the same finite-and-never-negative rule as every extent (`an_extent_can_be_in_viewport_units`, `a_viewport_extent_is_finite_checked_and_never_negative`). The issue also named `Dvh`; Bevy 0.20 has no dynamic-viewport unit, so there is none to write.
1. **A border in logical px: `BorderPx(Sides<f32>)`.** `Shape::border` is in line weights of `GooeySkin::border` and **is snapped to whole device pixels**, like every border `apply_skin` draws from the skin: at a window scale of 1.25 the 2 px line is 2.5 device px and is drawn as 2 (1.6 logical px), which keeps one weight on every side of a box; a protected editor test (`the_drag_box_is_plainly_visible`) wants at least 3 px, which a skin weight cannot promise. **What snaps (`snap_line_width`):** the frame of every widget role (button, panel, band, list row's line and rail, field, meter and the rest), the focus ring, the edge tab's edge and a `Shape` border weight. **What does not:** a `BorderPx` (the logical px it says; Bevy's layout then rounds it to its own device pixels like any `Node` border, which is the one rounding left), and a `Node::border` the host writes itself, which the skin does not touch unless a `Shape` or `BorderPx` names that side. `BorderPx` is a sibling component of `Shape`, not a field of it: `Shape` is built by struct literals (tests and hosts name all four of its fields), so a fifth field would break every one of them. Per side, an `Option`: `None` or a number that is not finite leaves the side, below zero is none; a side given here wins over a `Shape` weight on the same side; the colour is the `Shape`'s `ink`, else the outline unless the role has an edge of its own (a focused widget's ring still wins). `a_border_px_is_not_snapped_to_device_pixels_and_a_shape_border_is` runs at 1.25x and reads 1.6 for a weight of 1 and exactly 3 for `BorderPx(3.0)`, in the skin's outline.
3. **A button box: `button_box()`.** The editor's launcher cards and kit rows are a Bevy `Button` + `Role::Button` skeleton holding a picture, two texts and a badge, where gooey's `button` always makes its own label. `button_box()` is that skeleton as a bundle: Bevy's `Button` (so focus, `Activate`, the press), `Role::Button` (so a button's frame, fill, hover, press, focus ring and states, with nothing new for `apply_skin`), `Hovered`, `TabIndex`, directional navigation, laid out as a row with its children from the left (a `button`'s Node centres its label; this one does not) and `align_items` centre. **It puts `Role::ButtonText` on nothing**: a host's tests count `ButtonText` over the world as "a dialog's answers", and its children are the host's (`a_button_box_holds_children_and_has_no_button_text` walks the subtree). Words that should follow the state are a `fill_text` or `count_text` (their ink follows a button parent and they are not `ButtonText`); a plain `label` keeps the text colour. No new `Role`, so nothing under Breaking; a `RowLayout` now applies to a `Role::Button` too, so a host sets `justify`, `align` or `direction` (`a_button_box_is_a_tab_stop_and_takes_a_row_layout`; Tab reaches it).
4. **A hideable quiet status item: `status_item_quiet(text)`.** `jobs.rs` and `wish_view.rs` kept a `Node { display: None }` + `Role::StatusItem` off the `Activate` bus by hand. `status_item_quiet` is a `status_item` (the mark, the words, `StatusItem`, `Hovered`, a `StatusKind` that defaults to Info) without Bevy's `Button`, a tab stop or directional navigation, exactly as `plain_button` is to `button`. `Shown` hides it: `(status_item_quiet("Jobs"), Shown(false))` is `Display::None` and `Visibility::Hidden` (no room, nothing to focus), and the items after it close up (`a_quiet_status_item_can_be_hidden_with_shown_in_a_bar`, inside a real `status_bar`). **The documented way to toggle a node that already exists** is to insert or change its `Shown` (any node with a `Role` takes it; the test hides and shows an item that was shown at spawn): the `Shown` docs say so.
7. **A per-node colour override: `InkOverride { fill, ink, edge }`.** The editor's chips, buttons and list rows carry their own fill, ink and edge colours from a colour system of its own (`RowRest`, `Ground`) that gooey's skin overwrote every restyle, so a node could not be both gooey's layout and the editor's colours. `InkOverride` is a public component of three `Option<Color>`s, honoured for **any** `Role`: `fill` replaces the background (over a `Shape` fill and the role's), `ink` replaces the text colour of a text node's own role (a `label`, a `fill_text`, a button's `Role::ButtonText`; it does not cascade, each text carries its own), `edge` replaces the border colour (over a `Shape` ink; a focused widget's focus ring still wins, as it does over a `Shape`). A field left `None` leaves the role's own colour (`an_ink_override_replaces_only_the_colours_it_names`), it survives a skin change, which restyles every node (same test), it beats a `Shape` and loses to the ring (`an_ink_override_beats_a_shape_and_the_focus_ring_beats_it`), and a change restyles in the same frame while clearing a field gives the role's colour back (`a_changed_ink_override_restyles_and_none_gives_the_role_back`). It is constant across states: a host that colours by state changes the component. It sets colours only; size, border width and spacing stay `Sizing`, `Shape`, `BorderPx` and `Spacing`.
6. **Geometry rewritten every frame: measured, and a light path.** The compass gizmo, badges' layers and leaders, a terminal's rows and its caret are moved by a system every frame. The question was whether writing `PlacedAt` and `Sizing` each frame is as cheap as writing `Node`. **It is not.** Measured, with the timing printed and the counts asserted, by `writing_only_what_moved_writes_a_tenth_as_many_components` (500 nodes moved a frame, an unoptimized `dev` build on an Apple M-series, median of 15 frames after warm-up; the ratio is what is stable, the absolute number is the machine's):

| how the 500 nodes are moved | per frame |
|---|---|
| `Node` written for every node | 5.5 ms |
| `PlacedAt` + `Sizing` written for every node | 9.2 ms |
| `Node` written for only the tenth that moved | 5.1 ms |
| `PlacedAt` + `Sizing` written for only the tenth that moved | 5.5 ms |
| `HostGeometry` + `Node` written for every node | 5.5 ms |

A changed component makes `apply_skin` restyle that node (the whole `restyle` function, about 7 microseconds a node here), so a node that moves **every** frame costs about two thirds more per frame through the components than through `Node`; with a change gate (write a component only when its value differs, and only for the nodes that moved) the gap is a tenth of a millisecond for 500. **Guidance:** (1) write `PlacedAt`/`Sizing` for the nodes that moved and only when the value differs (`if at.0 != inset { at.0 = inset }`, as the test's system does); (2) a node that moves every frame (a gizmo's arms and dots, a caret, a terminal's rows during a scroll) writes its own `Node` and carries **`HostGeometry`**, a marker that makes `apply_skin` leave alone exactly the insets a `PlacedAt` writes on a `Role::Placed` node and the lengths a `Sizing` writes, and still write everything else (border, radius, padding, colours, overflow, and the widths a role, a `CardWidth`, an `AspectBox` or a `BoxSize` gives: those are not guarded); it has no change trigger, so add it at spawn, which also keeps a skin change from resetting the geometry the host wrote (`host_geometry_survives_a_restyle_and_the_skin_still_writes_the_rest`; without the marker the components own those fields again). The lighter component the issue asked for is therefore a marker and not a second set of position fields, so there is one place for each value.

## Review notes (the first cut of G-86)

- **The cost test asserts counts, not time.** `writing_only_what_moved_writes_a_tenth_as_many_components` counts, in a system right after the mover, how many nodes had `Node` or `PlacedAt` change in a frame (500 for writing every node, 50 for the gated movers) and prints the timing; a wall-clock ratio could flake on a loaded host. The table above is one measurement on one machine.
- **`button_box` has no name of its own.** It is named by a `fill_text`, `count_text` or `ButtonText` child, else by a `Tooltip` (`a_button_box_is_named_by_its_fill_text_or_its_tooltip`); one that holds only a picture and plain labels is unnamed.
- **`InkOverride` and states.** The `fill` replaces the role's whole fill chain, so hover and press change nothing under it: the host owns that feedback (`under_an_ink_override_fill_press_does_not_change_the_fill`, press standing for hover because Bevy's picking rewrites `Hovered` every frame).
- **The ring.** An edge override (and a `Shape` ink) yields only to the focus ring, recognised as the focus-ring colour on the focused node, not to any edge the role has (`an_edge_override_yields_only_to_the_ring_not_to_any_role_edge`: a dialog's panel has the outline, which is replaced). A role whose ring is its border (a `button`) has no ring if a `BorderPx` or `Shape` weight of 0 zeroes its width: do not zero such a border.
