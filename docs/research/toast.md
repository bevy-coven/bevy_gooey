## Craft principle

XAG 116 scopes itself to this widget: "The game includes on screen tutorials, notifications, or chat communication windows on screen that no longer appear after an allotted time." Its rule for an element that leaves on a timer: "The player is able to adjust the number of seconds that these elements appear on screen before encountering them (such as in a settings menu), up to at least ten times the length of the default limit." So a toast's time is not one constant the game hard-codes: it is derived from the message (a longer line takes longer to read) and multiplied by a number the player owns, and the multiplier must reach at least 10 without the widget breaking.

WCAG 4.1.3 on what kind of message this is: its "intent … is to make users aware of important changes in content that are not given focus, and to do so in a way that doesn't unnecessarily interrupt their work", for a message that "provides information to the user on the success or results of an action, on the waiting state of an application, on the progress of a process, or on the existence of errors". A toast is that message ("Game saved", "Inventory full", "Saving…"), so it never takes focus and never moves the player's: the focus ring, the pad and the pointer are where they were.

## How shipping games do it

XAG 116's own examples are shipped games, all adjustable: "In Halo Infinite, the player can adjust how long text chats will display in the HUD, anywhere from 5 to 120 seconds." "In Gears 5, players can adjust the amount of time, up to 60 seconds, that text chat will stay on screen after receiving the message." "In The Outer Worlds, players can adjust how long notifications will stay on screen." The Outer Worlds' slider is a "Notification Duration" in its UI settings tab, the shape a game using this crate gives its own settings screen: a slider bound to one number.

Sanctuary builds the rest by hand. `hud.rs`: "How many toasts stand at once (decision 0147: two, one line each)", `Toasts::push(text)` from anywhere (`Option<ResMut<Toasts>>` in eight files), `tick` ages them and drops the spent, `alpha` fades the last half second, a plate that "wraps in a plate of a definite width; a short one fits its words", a `SlideIn` as one appears, bottom centre, "newest lowest". And its rule for what a toast is not: "**A toast takes no keys**, so one that tells the player to press something is an instruction the surface cannot honour". The owner's decision 0147 ("too much UI at once") caps the stack at two.

## Model adopted

- **T1. A toast is a bundle you spawn: `commands.spawn(toast("Game saved"))`.** `toast_alert(text)` is the same for a refusal or a failure ("Inventory full"). Epic's `FSlateNotificationManager::Get().AddNotification(Info)` and Sanctuary's `Toasts::push` are one call from anywhere; this is one spawn from anywhere, with no resource to take, no stack to find and no layout to write (Buitinck et al.: "Sensible defaults. Whenever an operation requires a user-defined parameter, an appropriate default value is defined by the library."). The entity is the toast, so a game cancels one by despawning it. The first toast spawns the one [`ToastStack`] (a full-window, pointer-transparent column at `GlobalZIndex(150)`, above a dialog's scrim at 100 so "Settings saved" is readable over the dialog that caused it, and below a tooltip at 200) and every `Toast` becomes its child through a component hook, in the frame it is spawned, so it never draws unparented.
- **T2. The time comes from the message and the player.** `hold = (base_seconds + characters ÷ chars_per_second) × duration_scale`, then a fade out. Netflix holds timed text to "Adult programs: Up to 20 characters per second" and "Children's programs: Up to 17 characters per second", so 20 is the default reading speed; `base_seconds` (2.0) is the time to notice a thing at the edge of the eye and look at it. `duration_scale` is XAG 116's player option: any positive number works, a settings screen offers 1 to 10. Epic's `ExpireDuration`, `FadeInDuration` and `FadeOutDuration` are the same three phases; `bFireAndForget` ("When true the notification will automatically time out after the expire duration") is the only mode here, and a game that wants one to stay until something is done holds the entity and despawns it.
- **T3. A stack holds `max_visible` (3).** A fourth toast sends the oldest into its fade at once, not out with a pop. Sanctuary's two is the owner's own rule for that game; three is the default so a message is not cut short at its second sibling, and a game sets its own.
- **T4. A toast takes no input.** Every node in it is `Pickable::IGNORE`; it has no `Role::Button`, no tab stop, no `UiFeedback`, and nothing in it writes `InputFocus`. Arrow keys, Tab, the D-pad and clicks go to what is under it. It runs on `Time<Real>`, so a paused game's menu still ages its toasts.
- **T5. Where it stands is a setting, `ToastAnchor`** (six places, bottom centre by default, the place Sanctuary puts it). The newest stands nearest the screen's edge, and a new one pushes the older ones inward as its room opens.
- **T6. It is a framed panel with a mark.** `solid_surface` fill, `outline` border one snapped line wide, `radius`, body text. The first capture (2026-10-31) used the tooltip's `surface_raised` border and no toast could be told from the panel behind it: the fill is the panel's own colour, so the frame is all that separates them, and `outline` is the colour the meter, the switch and the rail are framed in, at 3:1 on the surface (WCAG 1.4.11). No shadow: the body is clipped to its room, which would clip one. A mark down its left edge in `accent` for `toast` and `danger` for `toast_alert`, four lines wide for news and eight for an alert: in the parchment skin `accent` and `danger` are two reds, so the kind is told by the mark's width first and its colour second, as a switch's state is its knob's place (`docs/research/controls.md`), and the words carry it too. A line wraps at the tooltip's measure (24 text sizes, about 40 characters; Netflix's limit is "42 characters per line"). The mark's two colours join `GooeySkin::problems()` at 3:1 on the toast's surface.
- **T7. Motion is the room opening, and one number turns it off.** The first build lifted a toast `gap` pixels into place and the older rows jumped a whole row (54 px at 720p) in the frame a new one arrived. Now a toast is a clipped column whose height, the room it holds, opens from nothing to its body's height while it fades in and closes while it fades out; the toasts round it are laid out around a height that is changing, so they glide, whatever came or went. The body's height is measured from its own `ComputedNode` (it is laid out whole inside the clipped room) while the room moves, and written as `max_height` by `apply_skin`. The room opens on the envelope eased out and closes at the end of the fade (`model::toast_grow`), so a new toast makes room while it is still faint and a leaving one holds its room until it has all but gone. `fade_seconds = 0` removes the fade and the room's motion for a player who wants none (XAG's reduced-motion line): a toast then appears and goes.

## Parameters

|setting|default|why|
|---|---|---|
|`ToastSettings::base_seconds`|2.0|time to notice and look; a design default (Android's short toast is 2 s, its long 3.5 s)|
|`ToastSettings::chars_per_second`|20.0|Netflix, adult programs|
|`ToastSettings::duration_scale`|1.0|XAG 116: the player's multiplier, up to 10 or more|
|`ToastSettings::fade_seconds`|0.3|fade in with the room opening, and fade out with it closing; a design default (Epic leaves all three to the caller)|
|`ToastSettings::max_visible`|3|a design default; Sanctuary's rule is 2|
|`ToastSettings::anchor`|bottom centre|Sanctuary|

Sizes derive from the skin: the stack pads its sides `padding × 2` and its ends that less half a `gap`, and each body has half a `gap` of vertical margin, so the stack's edge margin is `padding × 2` all round and rows stand a `gap` apart; the body's padding is a tooltip's; its mark is four snapped lines wide (eight for an alert). No `GooeySkin` field is added.

## Validation targets

- `model::toast_hold`: 10 characters at the defaults is 2.5 s, 100 characters 7.0 s, and `duration_scale` 10 gives ten times that; an empty line, a zero or negative reading speed, a non-finite number or a negative scale never produce a negative, infinite or NaN hold.
- A spawned toast is the stack's child the frame it spawns, and exactly one stack exists after any number of toasts; the stack and every toast node are `Pickable::IGNORE`; a pointer press over a toast reaches the node under it, and an arrow key, Tab and the D-pad move focus exactly as without it; `InputFocus` and `InputFocusVisible` are untouched by a toast spawning, standing and leaving.
- It stands `hold` seconds on `Time<Real>` after its fade in, fades out in `fade_seconds`, then despawns; a paused `Time<Virtual>` does not stop it; the 4th toast sends the oldest into its fade in the frame it spawns; `duration_scale` changed while one stands lengthens it.
- Text at least 4.5:1 on the toast (the tooltip's 14.26:1 dark, the same fill); the mark at least 3:1 on its surface in both presets (`accent` and `danger` on `solid_surface`); the frame at least 3:1 (`outline` on the surface: 3.68:1 dark, 3.81:1 parchment); `GooeySkin::problems()` empty for both presets and the new pairs reported when broken.
- Layout at 1280x720 and 2560x1440: a toast inside the window with at least `padding × 2` from its edge, three stacked rows `gap` apart, wrapped at 24 text sizes, the mark flush to the toast's left edge and the full height of it, every line a whole-pixel weight at 1x to 3x (`tests/lines.rs`'s rule).
- No toast jumps: at 60 frames a second a new toast's arrival moves the older one up by the new body's height and a gap, over several frames, none of them more than 40 % of the move; a toast leaving moves the older one down by the same, in the same way; and the whole move is exactly the new body's height plus `gap`.
- Frame time: no system walks a toast's text or restyles a toast that is neither fading nor new (the hold phase writes nothing); the gallery's `mean_ms` unchanged within 0.5 ms with no toast and with three standing.

## What great looks like

A line of news that is simply there when it matters and gone when it has been read: a framed panel at the foot of the screen, one mark telling "this is information" from "this was refused" by its width as well as its colour, text you can read at couch distance, never in front of a button you were about to press and never taking one from you. It makes room as it fades in, holds for as long as the sentence takes to read, and fades out, closing the room; the toasts around it glide and never jump. Three at once are three even rows, newest at the bottom. Over a modal dialog it is still legible. The same toast in the parchment skin is the same family.

## Deviations

- Not adopted: a toast that stays until dismissed (XAG 116's alternative to adjusting the time). A toast takes no input, so there is nothing to dismiss it with; a game that needs a persistent message holds the entity and despawns it, or uses the `dialog`, which is the widget with focus.
- Not adopted this pass: a screen-reader announcement. WCAG 4.1.3 wants a live-region role so a message that takes no focus is still spoken; `bevy_a11y`'s `AccessibilityNode` needs the `accesskit` crate as a direct dependency, a change to this crate's dependency list that belongs to the accesskit-labels item already in the backlog (it serves the slider and toggle too). Until then a toast is visual only, and the README and rustdoc say so.
- Not adopted: an icon in the mark. The crate has no image assets and the host supplies none; the mark is a colour and the words carry the kind, which keeps the kind off colour alone.
- Not adopted: a message queue that waits for room. Sanctuary's `MorningNews` waits for a clear screen; that is the game's rule about when to tell, and the game spawns the toast when it decides.
- Not adopted: a shadow on the toast. It would be clipped by the room the body is clipped to, and the frame carries the separation on every skin; a tooltip's shadow in the dark skin is a black on a near-black and shows nothing either.
- `base_seconds`, `fade_seconds` and `max_visible` are design defaults, marked above.
