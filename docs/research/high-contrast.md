# A high-contrast skin and the theme row

Researched 2026-11-01 (iteration 21). No issue is open; this is the loop's own pick, the second slice of the log's "settings-screen kit" (`docs/research/options.md` named it as "not adopted" and the log's backlog listed it). Two presets ship, dark and parchment, and both meet WCAG's 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for components, which is the floor. A player with low vision asks for more than the floor, and a game that wants to offer it has to design a third palette that passes seven more checks than it will think of: that is what this crate can do once and prove. The slice is a preset, `GooeySkin::high_contrast()`, a check that holds any skin to the high-contrast bar, `GooeySkin::high_contrast_problems()`, and a one-line options row, `skin_preset(label)` (Dark, Parchment, High contrast), that edits the skin's colours and follows them, like `text_size` before it. home-still's distill answered one search ("high contrast mode interface low vision players", 2026-11-01) with one relevant paper (Lewandowska et al. below) and nothing on game menus; Microsoft's XAG 102, WCAG's *Understanding* pages and MDN were read from the web and are listed under `## Pending index`. Ownership: the skin is this crate's own data (`docs/research/gooey.md`), so the preset and the row are in scope; reading the operating system's contrast setting is the host's (Bevy 0.20 exposes none).

## Craft principle

Lewandowska et al. measured what a player sees at the edge of an interface: "In the case of angular distances, the least visible elements were located in the corners of the display, especially in the low contrast setup. Only about 3 out of 10 users saw them", and "The increase in the contrast level significantly improved the visibility of these elements, but still not at a satisfactory level." The corners are where this crate puts the legend, the toasts and the tooltips, and contrast is the one lever that moves how many players see them; it is also the lever a game cannot pull for one player without a setting. WCAG gives the number its reason: "The contrast ratio of 7:1 was chosen for level AAA because it compensated for the loss in contrast sensitivity usually experienced by users with vision loss equivalent to approximately 20/80 vision. ... The 7:1 level therefore generally provides compensation for the loss in contrast sensitivity experienced by users with low vision who do not use assistive technology and provides contrast enhancement for color deficiency as well." And it names the mechanism this crate builds, as a sufficient technique: "G174: Providing a control with a sufficient contrast ratio that allows users to switch to a presentation that uses sufficient contrast" and the reason it must be a *choice* and not a default: "Some people with cognitive disabilities require color combinations or hues that have low contrast, and therefore we allow and encourage authors to provide mechanisms to adjust the foreground and background colors of the content."

## How shipping games do it

Microsoft's XAG 102 states the rule for a game: "A high contrast mode (either light, dark, or both) should be provided. When enabled, the contrast ratios should equal or exceed **7:1** for all UI elements against their background." It says what a high-contrast mode is for, against the misconception that it only separates light from dark: "the true intent of high contrast modes include the following: Increase the visibility of important elements; Increase the visual separation between different types of important elements; Increase the visual separation between important elements and unimportant elements." Its general approaches are the palette's brief: "Provide players the option to put a solid background behind any on screen text", "Support a high contrast mode across different aspects of your game" and "Add borders around text or elements". Its shipped examples are the shape of the palette: "In The Outer Worlds, the white outline around the bright red and purple meters, as well as the bright yellow text against the opaque backgrounds of HUD elements, increase visibility" (white outlines, a bright accent, opaque backgrounds); Minecraft's "slider tabs are light gray with a black outline, which is visible against the dark gray background" (a control's part and its edge are different lights); Gears Tactics' slider tab "is a bright glowing white circle". For the operating system's own mode: "Where available, read the platform-provided contrast settings to determine whether high contrast modes should be turned on/off at game launch by default ... it should be enabled at the game launch until the player otherwise reconfigures the game settings."

## Model adopted

- **C1. A preset: `GooeySkin::high_contrast()`, dark.** XAG allows "either light, dark, or both"; one is shipped, dark (the crate's default look, so the player who needs more contrast changes the least). Opaque near-black surfaces (the Outer Worlds' "opaque backgrounds"), white text and white outlines, an amber accent on black (bright yellow-orange text and fills read on every surface), a cyan focus ring (a different hue and light from the accent, so a focused selected tab is not one amber blur), and a salmon `danger`: WCAG's own advisory is "avoiding red on black" because of protanopia ("the use of predominantly long wavelength colors against darker colors (generally appearing black)"), so the danger colour is a light red that holds 7:1 and stays distinct from the amber. Sizes are the other presets' (`sizes()`), so a layout never moves when the look changes.
- **C2. The bar is checkable: `GooeySkin::high_contrast_problems()`.** The same pairs `problems()` checks (text on each surface, accent text on accent, the ring, the accent, the outline and the danger colour on the surfaces they are drawn on) with every one held to 7:1, plus the non-colour rules (target size, sizes), so a game that writes its own high-contrast skin has the test XAG describes in one call. `problems()` is unchanged. `model::ENHANCED_CONTRAST` is 7.0 (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.6, XAG 102).
- **C3. A row is a `choice`, and the skin is the setting.** `skin_preset(label)` is `choice(label, ["Dark", "Parchment", "High contrast"], ..)` plus the `OptionRow::SkinPreset` marker, and follows `docs/research/options.md` C1 to C5: the state is derived from `GooeySkin` (the preset whose colours are nearest, so a skin a game tuned shows the nearest one and is not rewritten until the player steps), a step writes the preset's **colours and no other field** (the player's `text_scale` and a game's own sizes survive: the player who set 200% text and then asks for high contrast gets both), and a game's own write moves the row with no `ValueChange`. `SkinPreset` is `#[non_exhaustive]`, with `ALL`, `name()` and `skin()` (the whole preset) and `GooeySkin::with_colours_of` (take a preset's colours, keep the sizes).
- **C4. The frame is part of the look.** XAG: "Add borders around text or elements"; WCAG on boundaries: "For people with cognitive disabilities, it is a best practice to delineate the boundary of all controls, even those that have visible content". A capture of the palette alone showed the panels' and the tooltip's edge too faint, so one `Option<Color>` skin field, `surface_edge` (`None` keeps today's look, so the other presets and every saved skin are unchanged), draws the edge of a panel, a tooltip and the legend; a button keeps no frame at rest (see `## Deviations`).
- **C5. The host reads the system setting, and the crate gives it a hook.** Bevy 0.20 exposes no contrast preference, so at launch a host that learns the platform's setting inserts `GooeySkin::high_contrast()` (or `.with_colours_of`) and the row shows it in its first frame (`options.md` C3); the crate does not detect.

## Parameters

- `model::ENHANCED_CONTRAST = 7.0`: WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.6, XAG 102's "7:1 for all UI elements".
- The high-contrast palette (sRGB): background `#000000`, surface `#050505` (opaque), `surface_raised` `#222222` (as light as the hover mix allows: white text on it a third of the way to the accent is 7.3:1), text `#FFFFFF`, `text_muted` `#B8B8B8` (8.0:1 on the raised grey, 10.2:1 on the surface, plainly dimmer than the text), accent `#FFA800` amber (8.2:1 on the raised grey, 10.9:1 under black accent text), danger `#FF8080` (8.4:1 on the surface), focus ring `#33E5FF` (10.5:1 on the raised grey), outline and `surface_edge` `#FFFFFF` (20:1), scrim black at 0.8. The values are tuned against `high_contrast_problems()` and `tests/skin.rs` measures the drawn text; these are design defaults from the sources above, not measured ones.
- The row's three options and their order: Dark, Parchment, High contrast; the first two are the crate's existing presets, unchanged by this slice.

## Validation targets

- Every pair `problems()` checks is at least 7:1 in `GooeySkin::high_contrast()` (`tests/skin.rs`), and `high_contrast_problems()` is empty for it and reports every pair under 7:1 for dark and parchment (a test that can fail).
- The dark and parchment presets are unchanged to the pixel (the regression stills at 1280x720 and 2560x1440, both skins, 1.5x text).
- A step writes the colours and no other field (`text_scale`, a custom `gap`, the scale's reference and the binding prompts survive), one step one `ValueChange<usize>`; a skin set by the game moves the row with no event; a skin between presets shows the nearest; a row spawned over a loaded high-contrast skin shows it in its first frame (`tests/options.rs`).
- Measured on the captures: text on every surface at least 7:1 from the pixels (not only from the constants), the focus ring distinct from the accent and from the resting frame at both window sizes, a hit target at least 44 px, the dialog's panel separated from the scrim (the panel's frame against the dimmed screen at 3:1 or better), and nothing in the gallery drawn in a colour the skin does not name.
- Frame time within 0.5 ms of before (`apply_skin` reads the skin on its change flag; a preset is data).

## What great looks like

A black screen, white type and a few amber and cyan marks: nothing is grey on grey. The three rows of a panel read as controls at once: a toggle's track framed white with an amber fill when on, a slider's rail white with an amber fill and a white thumb, a choice's chevrons white, the selected tab an amber block with black type. The focus ring is a cyan frame no other state uses, visible on a button, a row and a field at 720p and 1440p. A dialog is a framed black card on a dimmed black screen and still reads as a layer. Stepping the Theme row from Dark to High contrast restyles the whole screen in one frame, at the player's own text size, and nothing moves by a pixel. The look is austere, as Windows' black theme and the Outer Worlds' HUD are, and not garish: three hues, no gradients, no greys that the XAG number would reject.

## Deviations

- XAG says "light, dark, or both"; this pass ships the dark preset only. A light high-contrast preset is a later slice if a game asks (a skin is plain data and `high_contrast_problems()` is the check).
- The preset names are English words ("Dark", "Parchment", "High contrast") where the other rows' options are numerals and proper nouns. A host that localises writes a `choice` over its own strings with `SkinPreset::ALL` and `GooeySkin::with_colours_of` (documented); a design default.
- The crate does not read the operating system's contrast setting (no Bevy API), which XAG asks games to do "where available".
- **A new skin field, `surface_edge: Option<Color>`.** The first capture of the palette alone (`/tmp/g21m/t/hc-focus.png`, removed) showed a tooltip over the Load game panel with nothing to tell the two black cards apart but a `#222222` edge at 1.3:1 to the surface: XAG's "Add borders around text or elements" and WCAG's "a best practice to delineate the boundary of all controls" asked for more than the palette could give, since `surface_raised` is capped dark by the hover mix (`text` on `surface_raised` a third of the way to the accent must be 7:1). The border of a panel, a tooltip and the legend is `surface_edge` when set and `surface_raised` when not, so the dark and parchment presets are unchanged to the pixel and a saved skin without the field reads `None`; `high_contrast()` sets white, 20:1. It makes `GooeySkin` one field longer, which is a breaking change for a literal that names every field (a minor bump before 1.0, in the changelog). A design choice.
- **A button at rest has no frame, in any preset.** WCAG 2.4.13 (Focus Appearance) asks for 3:1 between a focused and an unfocused control; a white frame at rest would leave the cyan ring 1.6:1 from it, hue alone. The focus ring is the one frame that appears, and the high-contrast buttons are identified by their text and fill (non-text contrast: "A boundary ... is only required when there is no other visual way to identify the presence of the control"). The cost is a button's fill against its panel, 1.3:1 (the dark preset's is 1.4:1), which the hover mix bounds: a lighter fill would fail 7:1 for white text on a hovered button. A design choice.
